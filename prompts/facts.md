STABLE BACKGROUND
- The Afghan Special Immigrant Visa (SIV) program was created by the Afghan Allies Protection Act of 2009 for Afghans who worked for or on behalf of the U.S. government in Afghanistan and faced threats because of it.
- SIV applicants must show qualifying employment, get a letter of recommendation, and receive Chief of Mission approval before the visa stage. Many have waited years.
- The U.S. military withdrawal from Afghanistan ended in August 2021. Many eligible allies and their families were left behind.
- P-1 is a U.S. refugee program referral made for a specific individual, for example by a U.S. embassy or a qualified NGO.
- P-2 is a group-based refugee access designation created in 2021 for Afghans who worked for U.S.-funded programs, U.S.-based NGOs, or U.S.-based media, and their families.
- P-1 and P-2 applicants generally must reach a third country to be processed, because there is no U.S. processing inside Afghanistan.
- Many Afghans who were evacuated in 2021 entered the U.S. on humanitarian parole, which is temporary and does not by itself provide a path to permanent status.
- The Afghan Adjustment Act is proposed legislation meant to give evacuated Afghans a path to permanent residency.
- Interpreters, drivers, guards, engineers, and other local staff took serious risks to support U.S. missions for nearly 20 years.
- U.S. veterans and veterans' groups have been some of the strongest voices for keeping the promise to Afghan allies.
